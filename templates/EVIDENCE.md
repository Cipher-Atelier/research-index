# Evidence record

Record what the source shows and how to locate it. Interpretation belongs in a separate field or linked hypothesis.

## Provenance and access

- Evidence identifier:
- Author or creator, title, and date where known:
- Institution, collection, shelfmark, or catalogue identifier:
- Stable source URL and access date:
- Exact page, folio, image, line, or coordinates:
- Item inspected: original / authorized scan / facsimile / transcription / quotation / description only
- File version or checksum, when a permitted local copy is used:
- Access gaps, image quality limits, or missing parts:

Distinguish a source's stated date or attribution from your own inference. A catalogue description is not a substitute for seeing the item.

## Observation and transcription

Record the relevant marks, text, or measurements. Preserve the source order, line boundaries when relevant, uncertain characters, alternatives, omissions, and damaged areas. Explain your notation. Keep the literal transcription separate from normalization, expansion, editorial repair, and translation. Link any permitted crops to their position in the full source.

## Interpretation and competing readings

State what this evidence may support and what it cannot establish. Link hypotheses, alternative readings, and any disagreement about transcription. Do not silently choose the version that makes the preferred result work.

## Credit and redistribution

- Provider and earlier researchers to cite:
- Rights statement or permission and its source:
- What may be redistributed, with any required credit:
- Material linked but not included:

Public availability does not establish permission to republish. If terms are unknown or restrictive, link the authorized provider and explain what reviewers must obtain themselves. Do not include private, sensitive, or restricted material without the appropriate authorization.

## Corrections

Record the date, change, reason, and previous version. Link any analyses affected by the correction.
