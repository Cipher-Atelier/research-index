# Research topic

## Question and current status

State the question, material, and limits of the investigation in a short paragraph.

- Proposal or coordinating issue:
- Current status: proposed / active / paused / concluded
- Evidence status: untested hypothesis / partial result / conditional result / reproduced result / independently reviewed result
- Latest update:

Explain what has actually been established and what remains unresolved. An activity status is not a verdict on the claim. Name the exact claim and review record behind any “reproduced” or “reviewed” description.

## Sources and prior work

List the institution or collection, catalogue identifier, stable link, and exact page or image. State what has been inspected and what remains unavailable. Credit previous editions, readings, keys, transcriptions, datasets, and code. Clearly identify any application or correction of a known key.

## What is in this repository

Link the source records, transcription, hypotheses, research log, code, reproduction instructions, and results that actually exist. Say which essential items are missing or cannot be distributed.

## Results and limits

Summarize the literal result, interpretation, and confidence separately. Identify uncertain signs, assumptions, editorial repairs, translations, unresolved values, and alternative explanations. Include important negative results. Do not describe coverage as accuracy or a finite search as excluding every possibility.

## Reproduce or review the work

Link a minimal procedure with the exact inputs, versions, and expected outputs. State source-access or environment limits. Summarize what another person has independently checked and what they have not. If reproduction is not yet possible, say what is needed.

## Help with the next step

List a few small, concrete open questions with links to issues. Contributors can coordinate in an issue, fork the public repository, and submit a PR without organization membership. Constructive challenges and independent replications are welcome.

## Credit and permissions

Credit contributors by their chosen public attribution and describe their contributions. Record relevant AI tools and how their output was checked. Preserve each source's rights and permissions; identify which licence or terms apply to each class of material. Do not imply that the repository's licence covers third-party scans. If rights are unclear, link the authorized source and state the gap.
