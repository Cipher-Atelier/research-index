# Rome: fixed Berlin-key transfer, 9 October 2026

The completed test of transferring the fixed Berlin key to the Rome telegram was negative. Of 99 positions, 87 received a mapped value and 12 remained masked. Coverage records assigned positions; it is not an accuracy score.

The saved-data replay passed in the completed residual-evidence audit. That verifies the recorded computation. It does not establish a readable Rome plaintext, validate the source transcription or exclude every cipher model. The negative conclusion applies only to this fixed-key transfer under its stated input and masks. Other keys, models and source alternatives remain open.

No new letters or a Rome key were established. A further experiment needs a bounded hypothesis, fixed source alternatives and an independent check planned before evaluation. None is launched here.

Antal, Zajac and Grošek's [HistoCrypt 2020 paper](https://ep.liu.se/ecp/171/004/ecp2020_171_004.pdf) gives background on Slovak diplomatic ciphers and examples including Rome. It is prior scholarship, not evidence for the 99-position calculation reported here.

This summary reports the completed check supplied for publication and checked against its saved completion record. The underlying new audit scripts and full package are not included and were not rerun during this publication pass. This documentation is hosted in the research index; no dedicated Rome repository has been created. Codex assisted the summary and evidence checks. No source images, crops or private correspondence are reproduced.
