# Start here

Cipher-Atelier is an informal research community founded by [Maxim Egorov](https://github.com/cayde-6). We study questions that can be investigated through sources, careful reasoning, and repeatable tests. The [research index](README.md) currently covers nine historical-cipher investigations; proposals beyond letters and ciphers are welcome when the question and evidence are clear.

Choose the route that fits your next step. Participation is free. You can open an issue, fork a public repository, and submit a pull request without joining the GitHub organization.

## Start a new investigation

1. Search the [index](README.md) and [open proposals](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index/issues) for related work. Read the relevant sources and credit earlier researchers.
2. Open a [research proposal](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index/issues/new?template=research-proposal.yml). Give the question, accessible sources or source gaps, a first test, and the help you need. A complete solution or claim of novelty is not required.
3. Maxim reviews the scope and decides whether to host a new public repository, connect the proposal to an existing project, ask a question, or decline it with a reason. Opening an issue does not create a repository or approve a result.
4. After approving the proposal, Maxim creates the public organization repository and links it in the issue. The initial README records the scope, sources, current uncertainty, credits, and a useful first task.
5. Fork that repository, work on a branch, and submit a focused pull request. Link the proposal and explain how your contribution can be checked. Add the new project to the index through a separate small PR or the provisioning PR.

Approval is for hosting and coordination. It does not certify a hypothesis, reserve a subject for one person, grant funding, or create organization membership.

## Join an existing investigation

1. Pick a project in the [index](README.md), then read its README, sources, research log, issues, and recent pull requests.
2. Choose a small contribution: check one source, compare an uncertain sign, reproduce a test, challenge an assumption, add prior work, improve code, or explain a limitation.
3. Comment on an existing relevant issue with your planned scope. If there is no suitable issue, use the project's **Work on existing research** form. In the index, you can use [this form](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index/issues/new?template=research.yml) and link the project.
4. Coordinate overlapping work and link related attempts. Constructive independent replication and alternative explanations are welcome; you do not need permission to inspect public evidence or test a claim.
5. Submit a focused PR from your fork. Describe what changed, show the evidence, and record the checks you actually performed. Reviewers can ask for revisions before a maintainer merges it.

For a large restructuring or a change to project scope, discuss it first. A small correction or useful source lead can go straight into an issue or PR. If you stop working on a task, leave a short note so someone else can pick it up.

## Improve the documentation

Fix a broken link, clarify an instruction, add a source citation, or make a limitation easier to understand. Small documentation changes can go directly through a PR; no proposal is needed. Link the affected passage and distinguish a wording correction from a change to the research claim. For a documentation-only PR, mark experiment questions as not applicable rather than inventing a test.

## What a useful research contribution contains

Keep the contribution as small as the question allows. Include the relevant parts:

- **Source:** stable reference, exact page or location, what you inspected, and access or redistribution limits.
- **Claim:** a clear observation or hypothesis with assumptions, alternatives, and uncertainty.
- **Check:** inputs, versions, procedure, expected and actual outputs, including failed tests.
- **Credit:** earlier readings, keys, code, data, and people whose work you used; disclose relevant AI assistance.

A partial reading, a failed hypothesis, or a correction can be valuable. Applying a known key is credited as such. Coverage is not accuracy; fluent text, round trips, and agreement among AI systems do not establish a historical reading. Independent review should identify who checked what and with which evidence.

Use only the templates you need:

- [Topic README](templates/TOPIC_README.md): a new project's front page
- [Research log](templates/RESEARCH_LOG.md): dated progress and decisions
- [Hypothesis](templates/HYPOTHESIS.md): a testable claim and its limits
- [Evidence record](templates/EVIDENCE.md): provenance, observations, and rights
- [Reproduction](templates/REPRODUCTION.md): a procedure someone else can run
- [Contribution review](templates/CONTRIBUTION_REVIEW.md): a reviewer's checks and remaining concerns

Short contributions can put these details directly in the issue or PR. You do not need six new files for every change.

## Membership, credit, and public participation

For founder contact or to apply for organization membership, email [Maxim Egorov privately](mailto:maxim.egorov.dev@gmail.com). Maxim reviews applications as the community organizer; a meaningful contribution may also lead to an invitation. Research proposals are public research discussions. Invitations are manual; membership does not automatically grant repository write or administrator access, and there is no promised review time.

In your private membership application, include your contact email address, a link to your GitHub profile, and a brief explanation of why you want to join. Send these details only by email; do not post your contact email in a public issue.

Contributors retain their authorship and existing rights. Credit the people and sources behind the work, and follow the applicable repository and item-specific terms. Hosting a file here does not give us rights to someone else's archival scan, text, or dataset. If redistribution permission is unclear, link to the authorized source instead. Do not post private correspondence, personal contact details, credentials, or restricted material in a public issue or PR.

Read the [contribution guide](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/.github/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) and [community code of conduct](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/.github/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). Maintainers can use the [triage and repository checklist](docs/MAINTAINER_CHECKLIST.md).

For an optional public introduction and research discussion, use the [community introduction](https://github.com/Cipher-Atelier/research-index/issues/new?template=join.yml). Membership applications use the private email route above.
